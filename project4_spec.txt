Core idea for this next project:
The robot should drive on its own while maintaining a target distance from a wall on either
the left or right side.
This teaches:
• closed-loop control
• sensor interpretation
• reactive autonomy
• state-based behavior
What the robot should do
You should write a program so the robot can:
• move forward autonomously
• detect a wall on one chosen side
• stay a set distance from that wall
• avoid crashing into obstacles in front
• recover if the wall is temporarily lost
This should be a clean jump from the LIDAR safety-stop project.
Project structure
Basic version
The robot should:
• look at the front distance
• look at the wall-side distance
• try to keep the wall-side distance near a target value, put upper and lower
thresholds so your robot is not having spasms.
• turn away if it gets too close to the wall
• turn towards if it gets too far from the wall
• stop or sharply turn if something is directly in front
Zones
You can divide the LIDAR into simple angle regions.
Example for right-wall follow:
• front: 340–20 degrees
• right: 250–290 degrees
• maybe front-right: 290–340 degrees, use front-right and back-right angles so you
can track the wall better.
You do not need fancy mapping. Just use minimum or average distance in each zone.
Simple behavior logic
Case 1: obstacle in front
If front is too close:
• stop forward motion
• turn left until front clears
Case 2: wall is too close
If distance is smaller than target:
• steer away a little
Case 3: wall is too far
If distance is larger than target:
• steer towards a little
Case 4: no wall detected on right
If the right side suddenly becomes very open:
• gently turn right to search for the wall
